# Acute Startle-Habituation Trajectories Classify Later Seizure-Like Activity After Blast Injury in Larval Zebrafish

Post-traumatic epilepsy can emerge after a clinically silent interval, yet scalable methods to identify early functional changes before recurrent seizure-like activity remain limited. To address this gap, I developed a low-cost behavioral-computational assay that quantifies acoustic startle habituation in larval zebrafish after blast injury. Across three clutches, 133 larvae contributed 650 sessions comprising 19,500 startle trials at baseline and 0.5, 1, 5, and 24 hours after injury. Each session was fit with a nonlinear exponential decay model to extract the habituation time constant, tau. Among 81 injured fish with complete trajectories, an L2-regularized logistic model combined pre-injury tau, within-fish changes at 0.5 and 24 hours, and injury dose to classify a later seizure-like behavioral outcome. Nested leave-one-clutch-out validation yielded a mean fold area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.849, a pooled out-of-fold area of 0.833, and 76.5% accuracy, with 66.7% sensitivity and 84.4% specificity. Classifier performance exceeded a within-clutch permutation null (p = 0.001). At 0.5 hours, low- and high-impact injury shifted tau in opposite directions (+3.74 versus -2.72 trials; Cohen's d = 2.19), while a dose-blind model retaining baseline and trajectory features remained predictive (mean fold area = 0.810), demonstrating that classification was not attributable to dose alone. In a separate molecular cohort, behaviorally defined high-risk pools showed 27.5% higher fosab expression than matched low-risk pools (nominal paired p = 0.029 on the log2 scale), although a clutch-aggregated sensitivity analysis was not significant (p = 0.104). Each behavioral session required approximately 0.90 operator-minutes per fish and $0.094 in consumables. This work identifies startle-habituation trajectories as a rapid candidate assay for post-injury neurological risk and provides a rigorous foundation for prospective replication with electrographic outcome confirmation.
