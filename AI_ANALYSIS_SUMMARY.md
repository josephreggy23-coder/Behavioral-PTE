Post-traumatic epilepsy can emerge after a clinically silent interval, yet scalable methods to identify early post-injury functional changes remain limited. This study evaluates a low-cost longitudinal assay that combines pressure-wave injury, ToxTrac v2.50 centroid tracking, and clutch-aware prediction in larval zebrafish. Wild-type AB larvae underwent sham, low-, or high-impact syringe pressure-wave exposure across three independent clutches. A custom infrared rig acquired H.264 video at 1280 × 720 pixels and 60 frames per second. The author-supplied ToxTrac procedure tracked one larva in each of 96 arenas and summed centroid displacement during 0–1000 ms after each of 30 dark flashes before injury and at 0.5, 1, 5, and 24 hours afterward. Responses were fit by nonlinear least squares to an exponential decay model, yielding the habituation time constant tau; larger tau denotes slower habituation. Across 133 larvae, 650 sessions yielded 19,500 trials. In 81 injured fish with complete pre-injury, 0.5-hour, and 24-hour trajectories, an L2-regularized logistic model combined baseline tau, within-fish changes at 0.5 and 24 hours, and injury dose to classify a supplied later seizure-like behavioral label. Nested leave-one-clutch-out validation achieved a mean-fold ROC AUC of 0.849, pooled out-of-fold AUC of 0.833, and 76.5% accuracy at a 0.50 threshold, with 66.7% sensitivity and 84.4% specificity. Within-clutch permutation testing that reran nested validation gave p = 0.001. At 0.5 hours, low- and high-impact injury produced opposing tau shifts (+3.74 versus −2.72 trials; d = 2.19, p < 0.0001), while a dose-blind behavioral model retained predictive performance (mean-fold AUC = 0.810), indicating predictive information beyond explicit dose encoding. In a non-overlapping molecular ID cohort, matched high-risk pools showed 27.5% higher *fosab* expression than low-risk pools (nominal paired p = 0.029); the clutch-aggregated sensitivity was p = 0.104. This provides cross-modal validation of the supplied risk strata, not external validation of the classifier or electrographic epilepsy. Sessions averaged 0.90 operator-minutes and $0.094 per fish. Dark-flash habituation kinetics offer a candidate behavioral assay for later seizure-like activity; the pipeline provides a reproducible framework for prospective electrographic validation.
